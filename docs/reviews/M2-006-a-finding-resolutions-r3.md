## The owner question (R2-03): answered, Option A

ADR 0006 §3 says a restriction must survive **any** write failure followed by a restart. Its three mechanisms (run marker, tmpfs ledger, restrictive write path) all need one successful write to leave evidence of the failure; when the ledger append, both ways of invalidating the ledger, the anchor and the journal all fail at once, registryd holds the denial in memory, retries every one of those writes on every later operation, serves nothing eligible and refuses to shut down clean — but a process killed before any of them succeeds leaves a same-boot restart with a complete-looking ledger and a `running` marker. A reboot contains. The author sees no design that closes this window from inside registryd (every candidate — a pre-written placeholder entry, a heartbeat, a second ledger copy — needs a write at the moment every write fails) and therefore puts the decision to the owner rather than deciding it:

- **Option A (recommended): record the limit in ADR 0006 as an erratum.** The guarantee holds against any failure of persistent storage; the ledger is memory-backed and its appends cannot fail for space once stage B (or M2-007) preallocates its reserve like the journal's; the simultaneous failure of a memory-backed append, its two invalidations, the anchor and the journal is a host fault outside the guarantee. Consequences stated: the window is the time between that failure and the first successful retry; a process death inside it loses that one restriction to a same-boot restart only; the retries, the refusal to serve and to shut down, and the status report (`unverifiable_owed`) are the mitigation; a reboot contains.
- **Option B: an external witness.** registryd refuses to continue after the compound failure and exits with a status that the supervisor (systemd, M2-007) records durably before restarting it — a mark made by another process on another path. This needs the service management of M2-007, an ADR for the supervisor's role, and still loses the restriction if the supervisor's mark fails too.
- **Option C: contain on every unclean same-boot restart** (drop the ledger replay row of the restart table). Closes the window at the price ADR 0006 §3 was designed to avoid: every crash forces every user through the recovery ceremony. Not recommended.

**Answer (owner, 2026-10-14): Option A.** The author drafted the erratum under that choice: ADR 0006 §3 **erratum E5** states that the guarantee holds whenever one of the restriction's four durable marks (ledger entry, validity invalidation, `unverifiable` marker, journal record) is made before the process dies, and excludes only the simultaneous failure of all four followed by a death before any retry and a same-boot restart; it states what is lost (only those restrictions), the window, the recovery (a reboot contains; an operator who sees `unverifiable_owed` reboots before restarting the service) and why the window is accepted. No mechanism, record or format changes. The reviewer is asked to review the implementation against the erratum, and the owner to confirm its wording.

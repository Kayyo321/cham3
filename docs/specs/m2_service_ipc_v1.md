# cham3 M2 service interfaces and IPC, version 1

- **Status:** APPROVED with G3–G5 (owner, 2026-10-12, after independent re-review bd081df). History: PROPOSED under G3 ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005_m2_identity_vault_ipc.md)), with storage/lifecycle operations from [ADR 0006](../decisions/0006_m2_storage_anchor_recovery.md) and [ADR 0007](../decisions/0007_m2_lifecycle_time_consumption.md); revision 2 after the independent review at 9cf882503848f8c6cc4938a588cc9fc062a9392d (R05 identity persistence, R08 size contract, R04/R11 restrictive and deletion operations); **revision 3** after the re-review at 336f13be6a19bb871ea956d64f9cacb0936fe028 (R13 recovery ceremony operations and scoped key resolution; R07-r2 network slot in `admit`/`lineage_head`; R09-r2 shared trusted-time offset); **revision 4** after the re-review at f451f2e346e6981b24802a7e9070ba09303c40d3 (R14: the authenticated, vaultd-only registryd operation `name_recovery_keys` that completes ceremony step 3, with durability and reconciliation rules). The text is unchanged by the approval; M2-004..M2-007 implement it.
- Wire encoding of every message: `cham3_dcbor_v1` ([wire format §2](m2_wire_format_v1.md)). Canonical service names keep PROJECT §14's hyphenated spelling at the process/package boundary.

## 1. Transport and peer authentication

- One `AF_UNIX` `SOCK_SEQPACKET` socket per service, systemd socket-activated: `/run/cham3/<service>.sock` (`identityd`, `vaultd`, `contractd`, `policyd`, `registryd`). Socket mode `0660`, owner the service user, group `_cham3-ipc`; `/run/cham3` is `0750 root:_cham3-ipc`.
- Each service runs as its own system user (`_cham3-identityd`, `_cham3-vaultd`, `_cham3-contractd`, `_cham3-policyd`, `_cham3-registryd`); service users are members of `_cham3-ipc`.
- The trusted development client `cham3-ctl` is installed `root:_cham3-ipc` mode `2755` (setgid). Its effective GID is `_cham3-ipc`; it never passes connected sockets to other processes. Setgid execution makes the process non-dumpable, so same-UID processes cannot ptrace it without `CAP_SYS_PTRACE`.
- On accept, the server reads `SO_PEERCRED` (PID, effective UID, effective GID at connect time) and maps it to exactly one role. Anything else is closed without a response:

| Role | Condition | Acts for |
|---|---|---|
| `service:<name>` | peer UID is that service's system user | the service |
| `trusted_client` | peer UID in the regular-user range (login.defs UID_MIN..UID_MAX, default 1000..60000) **and** peer GID is `_cham3-ipc` | the human UID = peer UID |
| `admin` | peer UID 0 | administrative operations only; never signing |
| `fixture_consumer` | peer UID is `_cham3-fixture` (installed only by the test-only package `cham3-core-test-fixtures`) | labeled M2 stand-in for launcherd/netd/brokers/sessiond |

A caller-supplied UID, user id, key id, program name or role field is never authentication. Ordinary same-UID processes fail at `connect()` (no `_cham3-ipc` permission) and, if they obtain a socket some other way, at role mapping.

## 2. Framing and limits

- One request per datagram, one response per datagram, at most 65 536 bytes; larger, malformed or non-canonical messages close the connection.
- **Size contract (R08):** a message embeds at most one signed object (≤ 61 440 bytes, [wire format §2.3](m2_wire_format_v1.md)) and at most 4 096 bytes of other content. Every operation that can return more (`history`, `export`, `identity_state`) is paginated: each response carries at most one page (≤ 16 records or one signed object) and an opaque `next` cursor (journal sequence); callers repeat with the cursor. No response ever needs to exceed the limit.
- Request: `{"op": tstr, "request_id": bstr .size 16, "args": {…}}`. Response: `{"request_id": …, "status": "ok" / "denied" / "error", ? "result": {…}, ? "error": tstr}`. `denied` is a policy/authorization outcome; `error` is a malformed or failed operation. Both are final; nothing is retried implicitly.
- Per service: at most 64 connections, 8 per peer UID, 30 s idle timeout, 10 s per-request deadline (vault signing: 60 s), bounded worker pool. Excess connections are refused (flooding denial).
- The vault receives datagrams into a locked secret region; passphrase bytes are never copied into ordinary buffers and are zeroized after use.

## 3. Operations

Arguments are closed maps; unknown arguments are errors. "Owner" means the request's `owner_uid` must equal the trusted client's peer UID.

### cham3-identityd

| Operation | Allowed roles | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `register_enrollment` | `service:vaultd` | Validate a self-signed identity enrollment and its persistent and temporary key certificates for `owner_uid`, then persist them through registryd `append_identity` (identityd keeps no store, R05). First enrollment for a UID only; a UID with a live identity is refused (identity replacement is `admin` `replace_identity`). |
| `register_certificate` / `register_invalidation` | `service:vaultd` | Rotation/invalidation signed by the user's current identity key, persisted through registryd; invalidation is restrictive (denied before durable, ADR 0006 §3, §5) and is accepted in every user state except deleted. While the user is contained a certificate is accepted **only** as the `recover_keys` step of the open ceremony (key epoch = the ceremony's epoch, ADR 0006 §10); otherwise `contained`. Re-registering an identical certificate that is already durable returns its journal sequence without a second record (idempotent, used by `recover_keys` reconciliation). |
| `resolve_key` | `service:*`, `fixture_consumer` | `key_id` → public key, owner `user_id`/UID, authority class, certificate validity window, key epoch, invalidation/retirement state, the journal sequence of the snapshot answered from, and a **scope**: `normal`; `recovery` (contained user, open ceremony: only the ceremony's named persistent key, and only for trunk `restore`/`revocation` commits whose `authorization.session_id` is the `recovery_id`; the identity key only for that ceremony's certificates and for invalidations); or `denied` with the layer-6 code (`key_invalidated`, `key_epoch_stale`, `key_retired`, `contained`, `quarantined`, `user_deleted`, `recovery_key_required`). Unknown or registry-unavailable → `denied`. Every caller enforces the scope (R13). |
| `user_identity` | `service:*`, `trusted_client` (own UID) | UID → `user_id`, current key ids, identity epoch. |
| `replace_identity` | `admin` | Marks the old identity replaced; a new enrollment creates a new `user_id`. Old grants are not carried over. |

### cham3-vaultd

| Operation | Allowed roles | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `create_user_keys` | `trusted_client` | Owner supplies a new passphrase. Generates identity, persistent and temporary keys in locked memory, writes encrypted key objects, signs enrollment and certificates, registers them with identityd. |
| `register_proposal` | `service:contractd` | Registers exact payload bytes for one signing operation: `proposal_id`, `owner_uid`, object kind, `key_id`, expected head, policy epoch, expiry (≤ 120 s). The vault decodes and checks the payload itself (schema, intrinsic rules, `actor.uid == owner_uid`, `key_id` is the owner's key of the class the commit type needs) and asks identityd `resolve_key`; it refuses unless the scope permits exactly this object (a contained user's proposal must be a ceremony-bound trunk `restore`/`revocation` under the named recovery key). Returns `approval_digest = SHA-256(to_be_signed)`. |
| `approve_and_sign` | `trusted_client` (owner) | `proposal_id`, `approval_digest` the client computed from the bytes it displayed, passphrase. Checks owner, unexpired, unused, digest equality, attempt limit; derives the KEK, decrypts the key into a secret region, signs the Sig_structure it builds itself, zeroizes, marks the proposal used, returns the envelope. |
| `rotate_key` / `invalidate_key` | `trusted_client` (owner) | Identity-key-signed certificate or invalidation after passphrase authentication; registered with identityd. `invalidate_key` is available in every state except deleted (narrowing). |
| `recover_keys` | `trusted_client` (owner) | Ceremony step 3 (ADR 0006 §10): `recovery_id`, passphrase. Requires an open ceremony for the caller with no named keys yet. In order: (1) generates **new** persistent and temporary keys in locked memory; (2) writes their encrypted objects and the identity-key-signed certificates (ceremony key epoch, `supersedes_key_id` = the replaced key) durably (temp/`fsync`/rename/directory `fsync`), tagged with the `recovery_id`; (3) registers both certificates through identityd `register_certificate`; (4) calls registryd `name_recovery_keys` with the two key ids. Reports success only after (4) answered `ok`. **Reconciliation (revision 4, R14)** after any failure, timeout or restart, one recovery operation per user at a time: ask registryd `recovery_status` for the `recovery_id`. Status unavailable → keep every object and retry later; the held keys are named → keep them (step 3 complete); other keys are named → erase the held objects (retired); nothing named and the ceremony still open → **resume with the held keys** (re-register their certificates, idempotent, then name them); nothing named and the ceremony no longer open → erase. New keys are generated only when no held object is usable (their certified predecessors are then retired by the naming record). Objects are erased only on a positive status answer, never on an error, so a durably named key is never destroyed. |
| `cancel_proposal` | `trusted_client` (owner), `service:contractd` | Discards a pending proposal. |

There is no operation that signs caller-chosen bytes, exports a private key or keeps a key unlocked between operations.

### cham3-contractd

| Operation | Allowed roles | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `prepare_proposal` | `trusted_client` (owner); later `service:portald`/`authui` | Builds the canonical payload from a structured request and the current registry head/state/epoch: trusted timestamp, random `request_id`, `initiating_program` from the caller role, actor/subject/key ids from identityd. Asks policyd for mandatory-policy eligibility; registers the proposal with vaultd. Returns `proposal_id`, exact protected header and payload bytes, `approval_digest`. Subject identity is an M2 **fixture** supplied by the trusted client until M3 attribution exists. With `recovery_id` (ceremony step 4) it builds one lineage's recovery `restore` (or `revocation`): parent and `restore_reference` = the contained trunk head, every suspended grant id removed, the user's selected subset re-added with new ids, `authorization.session_id` = `recovery_id`, signed by the named recovery key; the client offers lineages in bulk but each is its own proposal and approval. A network proposal that would conflict with the lineage's network slot (wire §6.12) is refused early; admission remains authoritative. |
| `submit_signed` | `trusted_client` (owner) | Verifies the envelope through all byte layers and key authority, then asks registryd to admit it against the expected head/epoch. Returns `commit_id` and `pending` status. |
| `verify_object` | `service:*`, `trusted_client` | Reports the byte-layer and key-authority result for an envelope; never an authorization decision. |
| `request_revocation` | `trusted_client` (owner) | Immediately asks registryd for a restriction of the named grants (ADR 0007), then prepares the signed revocation proposal. Restriction does not wait for approval. |

### cham3-policyd

| Operation | Allowed roles | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `evaluate` | `service:*`, `fixture_consumer` | Runtime identity (owner UID, `app_id`, installation, package identity, executable hash, session), resource category/id/operation. Returns `eligible` (with grant id and commit id), `denied` (with reason) or `unknown` (treated as denied). Never reports enforcement. |
| `network_mode` | `service:*`, `fixture_consumer` | Effective mode of a lineage from its single eligible network grant (ADR 0007 §1): `direct`, `tor` or `offline` (no eligible grant, or mandatory policy forbids the mode). Eligibility only, never enforcement. |
| `check_proposal` | `service:contractd` | Mandatory-policy check of a proposed state/delta before approval. |
| `policy_status` | `service:*`, `trusted_client` | Current epoch, minimum valid epoch, emergency restriction state, rollback-protection mode. M2-006 stage A: the epoch, the minimum valid epoch and the emergency restriction are registryd's persisted policy state (anchor; raised by configuration, never lowered), the emergency flag also covers policyd's configured overlay (everyone for `service:*`, the caller's UID for `trusted_client`), and the rollback-protection mode is the anchor's `degraded`/`independent` ([storage formats §3](m2_registry_storage_v1.md)). |
| `set_policy_epoch` / `set_emergency_restriction` | `admin` | Raise epoch / minimum valid epoch; set emergency restriction. Releasing an emergency restriction restores no grant by itself. M2-006 stage A: both are forwarded to registryd's operations of the same name. A raise and a set are restrictive changes (ADR 0006 §3: in force at once, ledger entry, anchor update; with the anchor update failed the change stays in force and pending — `restriction_not_durable`, `rollback_status.policy_pending` — and is published by the next successful anchor write of any user and by `shutdown`); a release is permissive: anchored first, in force only once durable, and a failed anchor update changes nothing (`restriction_not_durable`). A lower epoch or floor is accepted and changes nothing, `min_valid_epoch > epoch` is `malformed_request`; the reply is the resulting `policy_status`. |

### cham3-registryd

| Operation | Allowed roles | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `lineage_head` | `service:contractd`, `service:policyd` | Current trunk head, live temporary heads, states, epoch, authoritative subject, occupying network grant (wire §6.12), terminal records of the snapshot, containment head, the shared trusted-time offset of this boot (ADR 0007 §5). Refused (`storage_unavailable`) while the ledger has failed and while the user's last record is durable but not anchored (the read retries the anchor update first; nothing of an unanchored state is served, M2-006 stage A). |
| `append_identity` / `identity_state` | `service:identityd` | Persist a validated identity record (enrollment, certificate, invalidation, replacement) in the user's journal; page through a user's identity records for identityd's index rebuild. Any other record kind, including ceremony and key-naming records, is `malformed_request`. An identical, already durable object returns its sequence (idempotent). `identity_state` is refused (`storage_unavailable`) while the user's last record is durable but not anchored, like `lineage_head`; a key invalidation requested in that state is ledgered and kept pending behind the unanchored record (M2-006 stage A). |
| `admit` | `service:contractd` | Atomic admission (ADR 0006): expected head/epoch, parents, placement (wire §6.11), base state, delta, uniqueness, restore, subject binding, branch scope, delegation, recovery rules (ADR 0006 §10) and the lineage network slot (wire §6.12), all under the user lock immediately before the append; durable before reply; admitted as `pending`. |
| `restrict` | `service:contractd`, `service:policyd`, `service:identityd`, `admin` | Restrictive change (ADR 0006 §3): in-memory denial, restriction-ledger entry, durable record, anchor update, reply. Reports `restriction_not_durable` while denial is held only in memory and ledger — also when an earlier record of the user is still unanchored: the change is then ledgered and kept pending behind it, never dropped and never appended beyond the unanchored record, and every later operation of the user retries the anchor update first (M2-006 stage A). Lifecycle records (`expired` at or after the grant's expiration, `session_ended`, `restriction`) are validated (`lifecycle_rule`); each permanently ends the grant ids it names. |
| `time_sample` | `service:policyd` | Reports a trusted-time offset increase (`boot_id`, `boottime`, trusted value) seen by policyd; registryd takes the maximum, appends it to the ledger at once and to the journal with the next `time_floor` (ADR 0007 §5). policyd uses it only after the reply. |
| `reserve_use` / `complete_use` | `fixture_consumer` (M2), later brokers | One-shot reservation and outcome (ADR 0007). |
| `confirm_enforcement` | `fixture_consumer` (M2), later launcherd/netd/brokers | Pending → confirmed/failed for one domain, bound to `commit_id`, runtime id, domain and epoch. |
| `session_event` | `fixture_consumer` (M2), later sessiond | Session start/end. |
| `history` | `trusted_client` (own UID only), `admin` | Read-only lineage history, lifecycle records and audit gaps. |
| `recover_user` | `admin` | Quarantine exit (ADR 0006 §4): accepts the verified journal prefix and re-anchors; the user becomes **contained**, never active. Grants stay suspended; the recovery ceremony (ADR 0006 §10) follows. |
| `begin_recovery` | `trusted_client` (owner) under option C1; `admin` under C2 and after `recover_user` when the owner chooses C2 | Ceremony step 2 (ADR 0006 §10): contained user, no open ceremony, not deletion-pending. Appends `recovery_opened {recovery_id, key_epoch, mode, role}` durably and re-anchors; `key_epoch` exceeds every earlier key and ceremony epoch. |
| `name_recovery_keys` | `service:vaultd` | Completes ceremony step 3 (ADR 0006 §10; revision 4, R14). Closed arguments `owner_uid`, `recovery_id`, `persistent_key_id`, `temporary_key_id` — no record, record kind, epoch or retirement argument: registryd builds the `recovery_keys` record itself. Checks under the user lock, immediately before the append: the user is contained (not quarantined, deletion-pending or deleted) and the anchor's `identity_seq` equals the journal's; ceremony `recovery_id` is open and has named no keys; each key id has a durable, non-invalidated certificate of this user, signed by the current identity key, at exactly the ceremony's key epoch, of authority class persistent and temporary respectively. Then one durable `recovery_keys` record → anchor update → reply `ok`. Effect, only once anchored: the user's minimum signing key epoch becomes the ceremony epoch, every other contract key is retired (including certified, unnamed keys of an abandoned attempt) and the named persistent key gains the `recovery` scope of `resolve_key`. **Durability and reconciliation:** while the record is durable but unanchored it is the user's pending tail — every later operation for the user first completes that anchor update or fails `storage_unavailable`, nothing is admitted or served past it, and startup rolls it forward (ADR 0006 §4); a repeated call with the same key ids returns `ok` with `already_named` and appends nothing; different key ids, a closed or aborted ceremony, another user's or an unknown ceremony → `recovery_rule`. Any other caller → `role_denied`. |
| `recovery_status` | `trusted_client` (own UID), `admin`, `service:vaultd` | Ceremony state (open, closed, aborted), the keys a given `recovery_id` named — reported after close or abort as well, so vaultd never erases named keys — and lineages re-authorized or still suspended. Completes a pending anchor update first (`storage_unavailable` otherwise). |
| `close_recovery` | `trusted_client` (owner), `admin` | Ceremony step 5: requires named keys; appends `recovery_closed`; the user is no longer contained; lineages not re-authorized keep their suspended grants (permanently ended) and later need ordinary freshly approved commits. |
| `delete_user` | `admin` | Tombstone-first deletion (ADR 0006 §7): reports `deletion_pending` until the tombstone is `deleted`; never reports success earlier. |
| `rollback_status` | `service:*`, `trusted_client` | Anchor mode (`independent`/`degraded`), run-marker decision of this start (`resumed`/`ledger_replayed`/`contained`), quarantined users with their reason (`stale_journal`, `unexplained_records`, `chain_mismatch`, `identity_mismatch`, `replay_defect`, `lost_store`, `anchor_invalid`, `deletion_pending`, `deleted_user_files`, `anchored`), users whose journal is unusable for a store defect (`unusable`, with the reason), contained users, open ceremonies (stage C), users with a restrictive write not yet durable or a record not yet anchored (`pending_restrictions`), whether the ledger failed (no eligible decision is served), users whose reserve is below 1 MiB (`depleted_reserves`, until the status operations of stage B), the policy state in force and whether it is not yet in the anchor (`policy_pending`) ([storage formats §§5–6](m2_registry_storage_v1.md)). |

## 4. Error vocabulary

Byte-layer verification errors are the codes of [wire format §7](m2_wire_format_v1.md). IPC/service errors: `malformed_request`, `unknown_operation`, `role_denied`, `owner_mismatch`, `proposal_unknown`, `proposal_expired`, `proposal_used`, `approval_mismatch`, `authentication_failed`, `attempts_exhausted`, `protection_unavailable`, `stale_head`, `stale_epoch`, `duplicate_identifier`, `subject_binding_mismatch`, `object_too_large`, `quota_exhausted`, `storage_unavailable`, `restriction_not_durable`, `contained`, `quarantined`, `deletion_pending`, `deadline_exceeded`; revision 3: `network_grant_conflict`, `branch_rule`, `lifecycle_rule`, `recovery_rule`, `recovery_key_required`, `key_epoch_stale`, `key_retired`, `key_invalidated`, `user_deleted`; ADR 0007 §5 erratum E4 (2026-10-13): `timestamp_rule` (reported as `admission/timestamp_rule`; admission-only, no journal record).
